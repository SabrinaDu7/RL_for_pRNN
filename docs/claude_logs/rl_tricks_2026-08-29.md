# RL interventions: what is already here, what is missing, and how to test each

**Goal.** Decide which cheap, well-known RL-side changes actually make this project's
training more efficient, and specify a test for each whose answer is checkable rather
than plausible. Efficiency is measured in **gradient steps to a given representation
quality**, not wall-clock — the speed work is done and the axis is fixed
(`README.md` § Terminology).

## Status at HEAD, and why this revision matters

The `action_offset` work landed while this was being written, and it changes what these
interventions are *for*. Every finding below was re-verified against `9e58995`; all six
still hold, and two got sharper:

- **The project's open problem is now policy collapse**, not throughput.
  `docs/claude_logs/compaction-2026-08-29-action-offset.md` names the next tasks as
  seeding the spatial probe, *"find the largest `entropy_coef` that keeps the collapse

- 🔴 **§2's bug went from latent to live.** The new `parity` preset sets

## 1. Advantage and reward normalization

**Why.** This is the one I would do first, and the reason is that it is not really a
tuning change — it makes every other knob in the file mean a fixed thing.

*Confirmed absent.* `compute_gae` (`rl/update/advantage.py:52`) forms
`reward = rewards + k_int*int_rewards + k_curious*curious_rewards` raw, and
`ppo_clip_loss` (`rl/update/losses.py:48-52`) consumes `sb.advantage` raw. There is no
running standard deviation and no per-batch whitening anywhere in `rl/`.

*Confirmed by measurement* (2026-08-29, reproduction in §9a), 30 rollouts, real
500-unit pRNN, default L-room:

```
rollout   cur_r mean   adv std   |adv| mean   return mean   value mean
      0     0.036433  0.378331     0.524572      0.277822    -0.228578
      6     0.019200  0.165371     0.122911      0.476349     0.479189
     29     0.018916  0.158740     0.120477      0.502672     0.539131
```

The curiosity reward halves within 30 rollouts. The repo's own committed measurement
carries it further: the action-offset A/B's endpoint table gives converged prediction
loss of **0.00442 / 0.00403** at `action_offset=0` and **0.00576 / 0.00563** at offset 1,
over 89,980,928 environment steps (`docs/action-offset-ab-2026-08-29.md`, n=2) — roughly
7x below the initial value measured above. (`docs/prnn-io-alignment.md` reports the same
order from an earlier run but is declared **superseded** by that A/B doc and still awaits
rewriting; the numbers here come from the current one.) **The reward is the world model's loss, and the world model is
minimising it, so the reward scale is non-stationary by construction.**

**The consequence, stated as a ratio.** The entropy term's gradient scales as
`entropy_coef`; the policy term's scales as `|advantage|`. Their ratio is therefore
`entropy_coef / |adv|` ≈ **0.083 early, ~0.59 late** at `entropy_coef=0.01`. That is an
independent, mechanism-level derivation of the number already sitting in
`training/schedule.py:183-184` — *"0.01 pins entropy near its 1.98 maximum and crushes
mutual information to 0.015, the bonus being over half the learning signal."* Two routes,
same answer.

**And a third route reached it independently, from the collapse.** The action-offset
compaction explains why `action_offset=1` collapses where offset 0 does not, in exactly
these terms: measured at `e=0.001`, `advantages_std` is **0.05535 at offset 1 against
0.04595 at offset 0** (+20%), so *"the entropy bonus is a FIXED additive term, so it
covers 1.8% of offset 1's advantage scale against 2.2% of offset 0's — already weaker
against a sharper policy. At 0.01 it becomes ~22% and the collapse disappears."*

🔴 **That is the whole argument for §1, arrived at from the collapse rather than from the
reward decay.** A circuit change that moved the advantage scale by 20% moved the
effective entropy coefficient by 20% as a side effect, and the exploration behaviour
followed. Under whitened advantages that coupling does not exist: the two arms would have
been comparable at a shared coefficient, and the collapse — if it survived — would have
been attributable to the circuit rather than to a scale it happened to shift.

**So the compaction's task (2), *"find the largest `entropy_coef` that keeps the collapse
duty cycle at 0% while holding `MI_policy` near its 0.001 level"*, is a search this
intervention removes the need for.** Today that knee has to be re-found per offset, per
seed, and implicitly per point in training, because the denominator moves on all three
axes. Whitening pins the denominator at 1 and makes the coefficient transferable — which
is the difference between a tuned constant and a knob that means something.

**A subtlety that shapes the test.** The optimizer is Adam, which is invariant to a
global rescale of the gradient (up to `eps`). So normalizing advantages is *not*
equivalent to a learning-rate change. What it actually fixes is the **relative weight of
the policy term against the entropy and value terms**, which Adam cannot absorb. That
predicts the effect is small at `entropy_coef=0` and large at `entropy_coef=0.01` — and
makes a factorial the right design rather than a single arm.

**Literature.** Burda et al., *Large-Scale Study of Curiosity-Driven Learning* §2.2 —
the closest published analog (pure curiosity, prediction error as reward, PPO). Fetched
live 2026-08-29:

> "Since the reward function is non-stationary, it is useful to normalize the scale of
> the rewards so that the value function can learn quickly."

— implemented by "dividing the rewards by a running estimate of the standard deviation
of the sum of discounted rewards"; and

> "While training with PPO, we normalize the advantages in a batch to have a mean of 0
> and a standard deviation of 1."

Both are absent here.

### Methods

**Where the code would go.** Advantage whitening belongs in `rl/update/losses.py`
inside each loss (it is a property of the objective, not of the minibatching), or in
`rl/update/policy.py::_index_policy_batch` if it is to apply per-minibatch. Reward
normalization belongs in `rl/update/advantage.py::compute_gae`, which is the one place
the reward terms are combined. Neither needs a `prnn` change.

**Arms.** Run it on `parity` at `action_offset=1`, because that is the configuration
where the collapse reproduces 2/2 and therefore the one with a signal to move. A 2x2,
because the mechanism above predicts an interaction:

| | `entropy_coef = 0.001` | `entropy_coef = 0.01` |
|---|---|---|
| raw advantages | **baseline** — the arm that collapses (19.2% / 21.1% duty cycle) | the arm where collapse disappears but `MI_policy` falls to 0.04-0.05 |
| whitened advantages | where the predicted effect is largest | should now over-correct at a *lower* nominal value |

Then reward normalization as a third factor on the whitened arms only.

⚠️ `parity` sets `train_policy.cuda_graph=True`, so §2's gates must be in place first —
otherwise a whitening flag added to `loss_kwargs` is baked at capture exactly as
`entropy_coef` is, and the arms silently become one arm.

**Controls.**
- *Positive control (gates the implementation).* With reward normalization on, multiply
  `k_curious` by 10. The run must be **unchanged** — normalization absorbs it. If it is
  not, the normalizer is broken and no other result from it means anything.
- *Negative control.* The same `k_curious` x10 with normalization **off** must change
  behaviour substantially. If it does not, the curiosity reward is not driving the
  policy at all and the whole premise needs revisiting.
- *Extreme.* `k_curious = 0` with normalization on: the normalizer divides by a
  vanishing standard deviation. Confirm it degrades gracefully rather than producing
  `inf`; this is the arm that finds a missing epsilon.

**Result format.** One row per (arm, seed) with `policy_grad_steps` on the x-axis:
`entropy_bits`, `mi_policy_bits`, `loc_entropy`, `value_loss`, `grad_norm`, and
`wm_loss` read from `predictiveNet.TrainingSaver["loss"]` — the exact per-gradient-step
record, a pandas frame appended by `recordTrainingTrial`
(`prnn:predictiveNet.py:225,593`). Prefer it to wandb history, which is server-side
sampled rather than complete for long runs.

**The primary outcome is the collapse duty cycle** — % of updates with
`policy_entropy < 1.0` bits — because it is the one quantity in this project that
reproduces 2/2 across seeds, and because sRSA is unavailable until the probe is seeded.
Report `MI_policy` beside it, since the compaction's task is explicitly a trade between
the two.

**Hypothesized result.** Whitening should collapse the offset-0/offset-1 asymmetry: the
+20% `advantages_std` gap that made a fixed bonus 1.8%-vs-2.2% effective is divided out,
so both offsets should need the *same* coefficient. Concretely — at a matched
`entropy_coef`, the offset-1 duty cycle should fall from 19-21% toward offset 0's 0.0%
without the `MI_policy` crash that `entropy_coef=0.01` causes. If it does not, the
collapse is driven by something other than the entropy/advantage ratio, and the
compaction's mechanism paragraph is wrong — which would be the most valuable outcome here.

⚠️ **Whitening rescales `|adv|` from ~0.12 to ~0.8 — about 6.7x.** Every tuned
`entropy_coef` in `configs.py` and in `slurm/train_fast.sh` (`ENT` default `0.01`,
line 101) means something ~7x weaker afterwards. Re-tune in the same commit, or the
comparison is between two different experiments.

## 2. The entropy schedule

**Why.** Two separate findings, one of which is a live bug.

**It already exists.** `EntropySchedule` (`training/schedule.py:172-215`) is linear in
environment steps; `loop.py:226` applies it per update.

**It is dead whenever `train_policy.cuda_graph=True`.** `algo.py:423-427` builds
`loss_kwargs` as Python floats **once**, on the first update; `policy_graph.py:120`
bakes them into the capture; and `rl/update/policy.py:100-103` routes the graphed path to
`_update_policy_epochs_graphed`, which never re-reads `algo.entropy_coef`. Recorded in
`docs/invalid-runs.md` under `4cfd8cb`, **Status: OPEN**, and re-confirmed at HEAD by
reading those three lines. Same exposure: `clip_eps`, `value_loss_coef`, and both
optimizers' `lr`.

### 🔴 The exposure is no longer hypothetical

When this was first drafted, production was clear of it (`train_fast.sh` defaults
`PGRAPH=False`). At HEAD that is no longer true:

- The new `parity` preset sets `train_policy.cuda_graph=True` (`configs.py::_parity`),
  and it is the shape every `*-parity` run holds.
- `slurm/action_offset_ab.sh:107-108` passes `--train-policy.cuda-graph` and
  `--train-policy.entropy-coef "$ENT" $RAMP` **on the same command**, where `$RAMP` is
  `--train-policy.entropy-coef-final`. Commit `8d5fabf` added that fifth argument.

So **any arm launched by that script with a fifth argument ran with its coefficient
pinned at the update-1 value.** The run name is built as
`mila-off${OFFSET}-e${ENT}${ENT_FINAL:+to$ENT_FINAL}-s${SEED}` (line 93), which is
exactly the shape of `mila-off1-e0.001to0.01-s2` — so that run was a *flat 0.001* arm,
not a `0.001 → 0.01` ramp.

⚠️ **A conclusion currently rests on it.** The uncommitted edit to
`training/schedule.py` states *"🔴 BUT THE RAMP DOES NOT FIX IT, AND MAKES IT WORSE"* and
cites `mila-off1-e0.001to0.01-s2` — 13.3% of updates below 1.0 bits against flat 0.001's
1.9%, collapse onset at 35.2% of training rather than 80.2%, worst prediction loss of
every arm. If the ramp was pinned, that arm and the flat-0.001 arm had **the same
coefficient throughout**, so the difference between them cannot be the ramp; it is seed
or some other uncontrolled variable. The recommendation the edit draws from it — *"Prefer
a flat coefficient. 0.003 is the measured knee"* — may well be right, but not on this
evidence.

**And it is invisible.** `entropy_coef` is set at `loop.py:226` and **never logged** —
confirmed: no `entropy_coef` key anywhere in `training/logging.py`. There is no series in
wandb that would show the pinning, which is why a dead ramp produced a plausible-looking
result rather than an obvious one.

### Methods

**Fix the bug before running any schedule arm.** The fix is specified in the
`invalid-runs.md` entry: pass the scalar as a **0-dim device tensor** and mutate it with
`.fill_()` — a tensor is read at its address on every replay, a float is baked at
capture. The same entry records the mechanism as *"proven to work under capture but has
not landed."*

**Three gates, all of which should exist before the next ramp arm is launched:**
1. A capture-then-change-then-replay test asserting the loss actually changed — the
   natural home is beside `tests/test_cuda_graph_policy.py`. Without it the fix is
   unfalsifiable from outside, which is how the bug survived.
2. **Log `entropy_coef` per update.** One line in `training/logging.py`. A dead schedule
   should be visible on a wandb panel, not deducible from source.
3. A config-level guard: `entropy_coef_final is not None` together with
   `train_policy.cuda_graph` should raise until (1) lands, rather than silently produce
   a flat arm. `configs.py.__post_init__` already does this kind of check for
   `action_offset` and for `rollout_cuda_graph`.

**Then re-run the arm that was lost.** `mila-off1-e0.001to0.01-s2`, at the same seed, is
the direct test of whether the uncommitted conclusion survives a live ramp.

**Arms, for the direction question (run after §1).** Flat `0.001`, flat `0.003`, flat
`0.01`, ramp `0.001 → 0.01`, ramp `0.01 → 0.001`. The two ramps are each other's control
and the flats bound them; the flats are also the compaction's own "find the knee" task,
so this arm set answers both questions at once.

**Hypothesized result.** With §1 in place I expect the ramp direction to matter *less*
than it does today, because the effective coefficient's dominant driver was the shrinking
denominator rather than the numerator — and the knee at 0.003 should move, because
whitening rescales the denominator to 1. If a knee still has to be re-found per offset
after normalization, that falsifies §1's reading and is worth knowing.

## 3. The pRNN → observation readout

**Why.** This is the intervention with the most headroom and the least prior
examination, and it is the one that needs the `prnn` fork.

**What is there.** `prnn:Architectures.py:180`:

```python
self.outlayer = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(hidden_size, output_size, bias=False), nn.Sigmoid())
```

trained under `predMSE` = `nn.MSELoss` (`prnn:predictiveNet.py:122,208`). Confirmed at
runtime too, not only in the source: `predictiveNet.pRNN.outlayer[0].bias is None`
returns `True` on a constructed network (§9c).

**What it is asked to reproduce.** Measured over the **complete** observation bank
(every position x direction, so this is the full support of the target, not a sample),
on the `multienv` preset (reproduction §9b):

```
distinct RGB tile colours: 6        distinct scalar levels: 4
  0.2980  46.79%   0.5725  45.16%   0.6471  2.94%   0.5294  2.04% ...
scalar range [0.2980, 0.6471]   mean 0.4322   std 0.1393
best-constant-predictor MSE: 0.018614
```

The prediction target is 147 floats taking **four** values, spanning **35% of the
sigmoid's output range**. Three consequences follow, and they are separable:

**(a) `bias=False` is a defect.** The target's mean is 0.432 and the readout has no bias
to hold it. The network *can* synthesize a constant — the hidden state has a non-zero
mean — but only through weights on a vector that is **61.5% exactly zero** and changes
every timestep (measured: `SR` mean 0.375, std 0.709, 61.5% zeros; §9c). So the DC
offset is **state-coupled**: it drifts whenever the hidden state moves. A bias
decouples it for one parameter per output. This is textbook for a regression head with
a non-zero-mean target.

**(b) The sigmoid attenuates the readout ~4x.** Its slope at the target mean is
`0.432 x (1 - 0.432) = 0.2454`. This is *not* the usual saturation failure — I checked,
and the targets sit in the middle of the sigmoid, not the tails — but every readout
gradient is scaled by ~0.25 regardless.

**(c) The reward's dynamic range is compressed ~8x.** *Inferred:* the compression to
[0.298, 0.647] is a rendering artifact of `tile_size=1` (`envs/obs_bank.py:43`), where
MiniGrid downsamples a supersampled tile and blends it with the grid lines. *Confirmed:*
the range itself, and that the four levels are an affine image of the true palette — so
rescaling to fill [0,1] is **information-preserving**, and would multiply the MSE, and
hence the curiosity reward, by `(1/0.349)² ≈ 8.2`.

🔴 **The coupling that governs the whole section: the curiosity reward *is* this MSE**
(`models/prnn_adapter.py::prediction_mses`, `rl/update/rewards.py:44`). Any change to
the readout's scale or loss silently changes what `k_curious` and `entropy_coef` mean.
**This is the concrete reason §1 comes first** — it makes these changes separable
instead of confounded.

### Methods

Three changes of increasing size. I would test (a) alone first; it is one parameter.

| | change | where | information-preserving? |
|---|---|---|---|
| a | add the output bias | `prnn:Architectures.py:180` | yes — strictly more capacity |
| b | affine-rescale observations to fill [0,1] | `prnn:Shell.py` (`obs/255`) or `prnn_adapter.flat_obs_rows` | yes — affine on a 4-level alphabet |
| c | categorical head over the palette per tile | `prnn:Architectures.py` + a new loss | **no** — changes the objective and the reward |

**The metric must be chosen before (b), not after.** An affine rescale multiplies MSE by
a constant, so raw `wm_loss` is **incomparable across that change**. Use **fraction of
variance unexplained**, `FVU = MSE / var(target)`, which is invariant to affine
rescaling of the target. Against the numbers above, today's constant-predictor sits at
`FVU = 1.0` (`MSE 0.018614`) and a converged network at roughly `0.0044/0.0186 ≈ 0.24`
(offset 0) or `0.0057/0.0186 ≈ 0.31` (offset 1).

⚠️ **Do not compare prediction loss across `action_offset` values.** The A/B doc records
why: offset 1's segment *"has one MORE row to predict… and its row 0 has no preceding
action to condition on"*, so its ~35% higher loss is partly a different problem, not a
worse solution. Any readout arm must hold `action_offset` fixed.

**Controls.**
- *Positive control for (a).* Steps to reach the best-constant MSE, `0.018614`. A
  readout with a bias should reach it in far fewer gradient steps, because that solution
  costs it one parameter instead of a weight pattern over a sparse 500-vector.
- *Null hypothesis for (b), and this is what makes it a clean test.* Since the rescale
  is information-preserving, **FVU should be unchanged**. Any improvement is
  attributable purely to better use of the sigmoid's range — nothing else can explain
  it. Any *degradation* means the change interacted with something unmodelled.
- *Negative control.* Rescale the observations and **also** divide the curiosity reward
  by 8.2 to restore its old scale. If the RL-side metrics track the un-rescaled
  baseline, the effect was reward scale; if they track the rescaled arm, it was world-
  model optimization. This is the arm that separates the two mechanisms, and without it
  (b) is uninterpretable.

**Hypothesized result.** (a) reaches the constant-predictor level in noticeably fewer
pRNN gradient steps, with an asymptote that is unchanged or slightly better — the
capacity was always reachable, just expensively. (b) leaves FVU close to unchanged (the
null) while raising the reward scale ~8x, which is precisely why §1 must be in place
first. (c) I would not run until (a) and (b) have reported.

## 4. Random-policy pretraining

**Why.** It costs no new code, and it is the sister repo's original configuration —
`README.md:5` records that the upstream pRNN work used *"a fixed random action
selection"*. So this arm is also the historical baseline.

**It is already wired.** `AgentType.RANDOM` (`utils/enums.py:24`), dispatched at
`loop.py:228` to `algo.randomAgent_collect_exp_and_update` (`algo.py:459`), which
asserts `train_pN` and trains **only** the world model — no policy, no curiosity reward.
A two-phase run is directly expressible today with no edits: phase 1 with
`arch_policy.agent=RANDOM`, phase 2 with `run.prnn_ckpt=<phase-1 pRNN>`
(`training/setup.py:192`).

**The forward-biased categorical already exists.**
`RAND_ACT_PROBA = [0.15, 0.15, 0.6, 0.1]` over `left / right / forward / pickup`
(action space is `Discrete(4)`; the mapping is MiniGrid's `Actions` enum) — 60% forward.
`pickup` is a confirmed no-op **for both landmark affordances**: a landmark is painted
as `Floor` when walkable and as the fork's `Obstacle` when `impassable=True`
(`envs/layouts.py:682-683`), and `can_pickup()` is `False` on both — neither overrides
`WorldObj`'s default, unlike `Ball`, which does return `True`. So `pickup` is a dwell
action, not a bug, and it stays one if an impassable-landmark arm is run.

🔴 **Defect found while confirming this, and it is spreading.** `RAND_ACT_PROBA` had
three homes at `8a8b06e`; at HEAD it has **four** — `training/setup.py:39`,
`log_and_store/storage.py:26`, a bare literal at `evaluation/checkpoint_series.py:226`,
and now `PROBE_ACTION_P` at `evaluation/circuit_diagnostics.py:40`, added by the
action-offset work under a different name. One fact, four spellings, and the newest copy
is the one a diagnostic reads. It should have one owner before any arm depends on its
value — and the fourth copy means the next diagnostic will make a fifth.

**Costs, confirmed.** `algo.py:461` asserts `num_envs == 1`, and the path loops
`pN.collectObservationSequence` in Python per trial. So pretraining does not get the
device pool that `train_fast.sh` uses at `NUM_ENVS=128`, and is far slower per
environment step than the AC path. Budget accordingly.

### Methods

**Arms**, all at **matched total pRNN gradient steps** — otherwise this measures budget,
not pretraining:

- `AC-only` — the baseline, one phase.
- `random→AC` — phase 1 random for fraction *f* of the pRNN budget, phase 2 curious.
  Run at least two values of *f* (e.g. 0.1 and 0.5), because a single value cannot
  distinguish "pretraining helps" from "any warm start helps".
- `random-only` — *positive control and upper bound on the confound*: what the pRNN
  reaches with no curiosity at all. This is the sister repo's setting.

⚠️ A resumed run's `total_grad_steps` is the **grand total across both phases**, not the
size of phase 2 — `loop.py:212` raises rather than silently exiting, so this will fail
loudly if set wrong, but set it right.

**Hypothesized result, and the reason I rank this fourth despite it being free.** The
thesis of this repo is that the policy shapes the world model's training distribution.
Pretraining on random-walk data forms the representation under a *different*
distribution before curiosity ever acts. So I expect faster early policy learning (the
SR is informative at policy step 0) but **equal or worse** final representation quality —
and if that is what happens, the result is a statement about the project's central claim,
not a tuning outcome. That is why it needs the `random-only` control: without it, a null
result cannot be told apart from "curiosity never mattered".

⚠️ **This is the one intervention here that genuinely needs a representation metric**, so
it is blocked on the probe seeding named at the top — more so than the others, which can
report on `wm_loss`, collapse duty cycle and `MI_policy`. Do not run §4 before task (0).
A random-action agent is also exactly what `evaluation/probe.py` already uses for a
fixed-seed readout, which is the natural place to take the measurement from.

## 5. Policy output head initialization

**Why.** Cheap, standard, and almost certainly a null here — which is worth establishing
rather than assuming.

🔴 **The proposal's target does not exist.** There is no `logit_net`. The actor is
`self.actor = Sequential(Linear(504, 64), Tanh(), Linear(64, 4))`
(`models/policy.py:47-49`), so the output head is `actor[2]`. The proposed line is
`nn.init.uniform_(acmodel.actor[-1].weight, -0.1, 0.1)`.

**Why the naive arm would be uninterpretable.** `init_params` (`models/policy.py:21-27`)
already normalizes every Linear's rows to unit L2. So `uniform_(-0.1, 0.1)` is **both** a
different draw **and** a 2.17x shrink of the row norm; an arm that changes both cannot
attribute its result to either.

**Measured on the real `ACModelSR`** at `SR_size=500, with_CV=False` (§9d):

```
default (init_params)     row_norm 1.000   H = 1.847 bits   max_p 0.405
uniform_(-0.1, 0.1)       row_norm 0.457   H = 1.931 bits   max_p 0.381
orthogonal_(gain=0.01)    row_norm 0.010   H = 2.000 bits   max_p 0.252
```

With four actions the ceiling is 2 bits and the default already sits at 1.847. **The
entire intervention buys 0.15 bits.** This trick earns its reputation on large action
spaces with un-normalized heads; this project has neither.

### Methods

**The arms must separate direction from magnitude**, per the paragraph above: a 2x2 in
{row direction} x {row magnitude} — `uniform` and `orthogonal` each at both the
proposal's row norm (0.457) and the standard RL gain (0.01) — plus the untouched
default as the control.

**One requirement the harness has to meet.** Each arm must draw its head from a
**dedicated generator**, so that applying it consumes nothing from the global RNG
stream. Otherwise arms at the same seed diverge in action sampling, minibatch shuffles
and world-model noise as well as in the head weights, and the contrast is not the head
init. This is a property of `collect.py`, and it is the single thing most likely to be
got wrong.

**Where the field goes:** an initializer enum on `ArchPolicyCfg`, defaulting to today's
`init_params` behaviour (§7).

**Result format.** `entropy_pre_bits` (entropy under current parameters on the fresh
rollout, before the update) per update, plus the standard `UpdateLogs` row.

**Hypothesized result: a null.** I expect the arms to converge within ~200 policy
gradient steps out of 320,000 and show no lasting difference. Stating that in advance is
what makes a null informative here rather than a shrug.

## 6. Critic burn-in

**Why last.** The problem is real, sharply bounded, and mostly removed by §1.

**Confirmed real.** From the table in §1: the critic starts at `-0.229` against returns
of `+0.278`. That is why rollout 0 has `adv mean 0.506` against `adv std 0.378` — the
first advantage is almost entirely a constant offset, so that update raises the
log-probability of essentially *every* action taken.

**Confirmed bounded.** The critic tracks the return scale by rollout ~6 (≈3072
environment steps at the measured shape). The `reference` schedule, from
`TrainingSchedule.summary()` rather than arithmetic:

```
rollout: 8 episodes (8 envs x 1) = 2,048 env steps -> 10,000 rollouts
policy gradient steps: 320,000 (32/rollout, batch 256 x 4 epochs)
```

So the bad region is roughly **48 of 320,000** policy gradient steps — about 0.015% of
the run. *Inferred and unmeasured:* whether those first updates create a persistent
attractor. That is the only way this intervention pays, and it is exactly what the test
should target.

**Note the overlap with §1.** Whitening advantages subtracts the batch mean, which is
precisely the constant offset that makes rollout 0 pathological. Burn-in and
normalization are therefore **not independent** — burn-in must be tested *on top of*
normalization, or it will take credit for something §1 already fixed.

### Methods

**The intervention is a scalar on the policy term**, and `rl/update/policy.py` is
already loss-agnostic (it resolves `loss_fn` by name from `LOSSES`), so this is a
`policy_coef` argument to `ppo_clip_loss` — `1.0` normally, `0.0` during burn-in,
leaving a pure value regression. Make it a **0-dim device tensor**, not a float, for the
reason in §2: a float is baked into a captured graph, a tensor is read at its address
on every replay.

**The gate that must exist:** `policy_coef = 1.0` reproduces today's `ppo_clip_loss`
**bitwise**. Without it the burn-in arm and the baseline differ by an unknown amount
before burn-in even engages, and every number from the experiment is uninterpretable.

**Where the field goes:** `train_policy.critic_burn_in_steps: int = 0` (§7), where `0`
is today's behaviour and is also the baseline arm.

**Arms.** `K ∈ {0, 32, 256}` burn-in updates, **each crossed with normalization on/off**,
so the overlap above is measured rather than assumed. `K=0` is the baseline.

**Hypothesized result.** A null with normalization on. Possibly a small early gain with
normalization off, which would be a fact about §1 rather than about burn-in.

## 7. Where these experiments live: the questions repo, not `throwaway/`

**Nothing here reuses `throwaway/`.** That is the repo's own rule, not a preference:
*"If this script was always meant to be thrown away, it should live in `./throwaway/`,
and NO RESULT should depend on it because it will NOT BE TRACEABLE"* (`CLAUDE.md`).

The state of those files, confirmed rather than assumed:

- They cannot run. `grep -ln "hydra\|initialize_config_dir\|cfg.rl.frames" throwaway/*.py`
  matches `critic_burn_in.py`, `policy_head_init.py`,
  `orthogonal_init_early_training.py`, `lr_warmup.py` and
  `compare_ranking_statistics.py`. They call `initialize_config_dir(REPO / "Configs")`
  — `Configs/` is now `throwaway/hydra_era/Configs` — and read `cfg.rl.frames` and
  `cfg.predNet.seqdur`, both retired words (`README.md` § Terminology), through
  `rl/update/updater.py`, now `rl/update/policy.py`.
- `throwaway/critic_burn_in_out/` already holds committed results — `baseline`,
  `burnin8`, `burnin24` at three seeds each. **Do not read a conclusion out of them.**
  They were produced by a script the current tree cannot execute, so they cannot be
  regenerated or diffed; and *when* they were produced is not recoverable from the tree
  (`bcbc728` only records the commit that relocated the directory). Un-regenerable and
  undated is exactly the condition the rule above describes.

### The established way

The traceable harness is the sibling questions repository,
`../experiment-curiousgeorge`, which already holds `Q0`, `Q1` and `Q3`. Its README is
explicit that *"the unit of work is a question"* and that *"we should never have loose
scripts or scripts named differently than what's below for results generation."*
Scaffolding a question is one command, and hand-copying one is forbidden:

```bash
uv run exp new Q4 --n-experiments 2      # scaffolds instructions, results template, collect.py, analyze.py
uv run exp run   Q4                      # collect + analyze + render
uv run exp check Q4                      # recompute and diff against the committed values
```

The invariants that make this the right home for these arms: `collect.py` only collects
and caches, `analyse` computes and writes nothing, numbers are never typed by hand, and
every rendered document records the `rl-for-prnn` revision that produced it.

### The design consequence, and it is the important one

The questions repo consumes this repository as a **pinned git dependency**
(`experiment-curiousgeorge/pyproject.toml`, `rev = 5a171038`). A question therefore
cannot test a change that has not landed here — and it must not monkey-patch one in,
which is precisely what the `throwaway/` scripts did.

🔴 **So every intervention in this document has to be expressible as a typed field in
`curious_george/configs.py`, defaulting to today's behaviour.** An arm is then a config
value (`--train-policy.normalize-advantage`), not a patched function. That is a better
design than the throwaway approach on its own merits — it makes the arm reproducible
from the run's own `provenance.json`, and it keeps the golden fixture green (§8) — and
it is the only shape the questions repo can consume.

**There is now a worked example of exactly this pattern in-tree: `action_offset`.**
`ArchPrnnCfg.action_offset: int = 0` (`configs.py`) is one typed integer defaulting to
the old circuit, validated in `__post_init__`, with `tests/test_action_offset.py` pinning
the shift and `tests/golden` bitwise green at the default. The A/B then ran as
`main_train.py parity --arch-prnn.action-offset 1`, so the changed variable is visible on
the command line and recorded in provenance. Copy that shape for each field below.

Concretely, one field per intervention: advantage whitening and reward normalization
(§1), the readout bias and the observation rescale (§3), the head initializer (§5), and
the burn-in length (§6). §2 is a bug fix, not a field. §4 needs no new field at all.

### Suggested question boundaries

One question per *question*, not one per knob. These group by what they are actually
asking, and by their dependency order:

| QID | the question | experiments |
|---|---|---|
| `Q4` | Does fixing the reward scale reduce the gradient steps to a given representation? | §1's factorial; then §2's ramp direction, re-asked after normalization |
| `Q5` | Is the world model's readout the bottleneck on prediction quality? | §3(a) bias; §3(b) range rescale with its reward-scale control |
| `Q6` | Do the standard policy-side warm-start tricks matter here? | §5 head init; §6 critic burn-in; §4 random pretraining |

`Q4` gates the other two, for the reasons in §1 and §3. `Q6` is where I expect nulls,
which is a reason to run it — a documented null is a result — but not a reason to run
it first.

## 8. Gates, and the protocol for editing the pRNN fork

**The golden fixture stays green — if §7's config-gating is respected.**
`tests/golden/test_golden.py` compares the `rounds`, `acmodel_state` and `prnn_state`
sections bitwise, and **not** the config. So a new field that defaults to today's
behaviour moves no leaf and needs no re-capture. That is a second reason to gate every
arm behind a config field rather than editing a code path unconditionally.

The fixture must be re-captured (`tests/golden/capture_golden.py`) and a
`docs/invalid-runs.md` entry written only when a **default** changes — for instance if
normalization is eventually turned on by default. That file's `UpdateLogs` entry is the
model: name the moved leaves, their before/after values, and the direction the mechanism
predicts.

**Baseline before any of this.** The action-offset compaction records the gate at
`4386aa1` as **473 passed, 1 deselected** (up from 463, +7 in
`tests/test_action_offset.py` and +3 auto-parametrised over
`evaluation/circuit_diagnostics.py`), with `tests/golden` bitwise green at
`action_offset=0`. ⚠️ That is a number read from a document, not from a gate run in this
session, and HEAD has moved three commits past it — **re-run the gate and record its own
final output before the first edit.** "No regressions" only means something against a
number you captured yourself.

**The pin chain is three levels deep, and §3 moves the bottom of it:**

```
LevensteinLab/pRNN @ 163cb578          (RL_for_pRNN/pyproject.toml:111, branch sdu/rl-integration)
   └─ SabrinaDu7/RL_for_pRNN @ 5a171038 (experiment-curiousgeorge/pyproject.toml)
        └─ experiment-curiousgeorge     (the question, and the rendered result)
```

⚠️ The questions repo's pin `5a171038` is an ancestor of HEAD `9e58995` but **23 commits
behind** it — so it predates `action_offset`, the `parity` preset and
`circuit_diagnostics.py` entirely. Any question testing this work needs a pin bump
regardless of which intervention runs first.

The `prnn` pin, by contrast, is **untouched**: `pyproject.toml:111` still resolves to
`163cb578` in `uv.lock`, and the action-offset work deliberately took none of it —
*"No fork. No re-pin. The circuit needed neither."* §3 is still the only item here that
moves the bottom of the chain.

For §3 the loop is therefore: edit `../pRNN_new` (already at `163cb578`, the pinned rev)
→ run its own gates (`tests/test_predictivenet.py`, `test_batched_predict.py`,
`test_temporal_alignment.py`, `test_spatial_metrics.py`, `test_trainnet.py`) → push →
`uv sync` here to re-resolve → run this repo's world-model gates
(`tests/test_batched_wm.py`, `tests/test_batched_wm_forward.py`,
`tests/test_ckpt_interop.py`) and `tests/golden/` → land here → bump `rl-for-prnn` in
`experiment-curiousgeorge/pyproject.toml` → `uv run exp check` there.

⚠️ `experiment-curiousgeorge` pins `torch==2.8.0` deliberately, because analysis that
replays a checkpoint must run the torch the checkpoint was made under. Do not let a
re-sync float it.

🔴 **Checkpoint drift is the trap in §3(a), and it fails silently rather than loudly.**
A pRNN checkpoint is not a `state_dict`: `saveNet` does `pickle.dump(self, f)`
(`prnn:predictiveNet.py:761`) and `loadNet` does `pickle.load(f)`
(`prnn:predictiveNet.py:784`) — the whole live object graph.

So adding a bias to `outlayer` produces **no load error**. Unpickling restores the old
module's `_parameters` dict, in which `bias` is `None`, so an archived checkpoint keeps
running the old bias-free architecture while the source says otherwise. Nothing in
`curious_george/` asserts the loaded architecture (confirmed: no caller inspects the
restored module's shape; `tests/test_ckpt_interop.py` gates checkpoint *filenames* and
optimizer *param-group counts*, not module structure, and `tests/test_ckpts.py` only
gates that a checkpoint loads and runs).

The casualty is `evaluation/checkpoint_series.py`, which scores a run's archive offline,
one row per checkpoint: it would silently mix old bias-free nets and new biased ones in
a single developmental series.

**So §3(a) needs a new gate, written before the change**: assert at load that the
restored `outlayer` matches what the current class constructs, and fail loudly if not.
Pickling a live object graph as the checkpoint format is the underlying flaw here — it
couples every checkpoint to the class definition and tolerates architecture drift by
construction. Worth naming even if the fix is out of scope for this work.

**Prior art for a re-pin exists** and went cleanly: the `pRNN_new` migration held goldens
across the swap with a single intended dynamics delta. The same standard applies here —
one intended delta per re-pin, named in advance.

## 9. Reproducing the measurements in this document

**Do they still hold at HEAD?** Re-checked: the observation target, the `outlayer`
shape, the actor head shape and the absence of normalization are all unchanged at
`9e58995`. The rollout did change (`collect_rollout` tracker-reset and `init_sr`
head-direction fixes), but the A/B doc records both as *"invisible at offset 0, because
`init_sr` returns zeros there and never reads the observation"*, and every measurement
below ran at the default `action_offset=0`. They stand; re-run them if that default moves.

All measurements were taken 2026-08-29 at base commit `8a8b06e` on a local RTX 4060,
via one-off scripts in a scratchpad **outside** this repository, which were not kept and
were never committed. No result in this repository depends on them. They are described
below so the numbers are re-derivable rather than trusted — and none of them belongs in
a results document until it comes back through a question (§7).

- **(a) reward / advantage scale.** `small_config(num_envs=4, episodes_per_env=2,
  episode_steps=64, rollouts=30, hidden_size=500, noise_std=0.05, dropout=0.15)` →
  `setup_training` → loop `collect_experiences()` / `update_parameters()`, printing
  `algo.curious_rewards` and `exps.advantage / returnn / value` per rollout.
- **(b) observation target support.** `setup_envs(cfg)` with the `multienv` preset's
  `EnvCfg`, then `np.asarray(envs[0].env._bank)` — the precomputed
  `(position, direction) → observation` table, i.e. the complete support. Unique RGB
  triples and unique scalars over `bank / 255`.
- **(c) hidden-state statistics.** `exps.SR` from one rollout at `hidden_size=500`;
  `predictiveNet.pRNN.outlayer[0].bias is None` confirms the missing bias.
- **(d) head initialization.** Build `ACModelSR({'image': (7,7,3)}, Discrete(4),
  SR_size=500, with_CV=False, with_HD=True)` under a fixed seed, apply each
  initializer, and read `dist.entropy()` on `SR ~ U[0,1]`.
- **(e) schedules.** `TrainingSchedule.from_config(cfg).summary()` for the `reference`
  and `multienv` presets — never hand arithmetic.
